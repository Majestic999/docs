## Mounting the component on the breadboard

**Step 1:** Place the buzzer on the breadboard. 

- A standard breadboard has a grid of holes divided into rows (numbered) and columns (lettered).

- Identify the pins of the buzzer. The **longer pin** is the positive pin (+), while the **shorter pin** is the negative pin (-).

- Insert the longer positive pin into a specific row (e.g., row 15) and the shorter negative pin into another row (e.g., row 18).

Ensure they are not in the same row to prevent a short circuit.

![Buzzer fixed on breadboard](../../../assets/1.0/BUZZER/circuit_1.png).

_**NB:** Make sure you identify where the positive pin (+) and the negative pin (-) is connected to on the breadboard._

## WIRING THE CIRCUIT

**Step 2:** Connect the buzzer to the ESP32.

- Take a **jumper wire** and connect one end to the row containing the buzzer's positive pin (row 15).

Connect the other end of this jumper wire to **GPIO 4** on the ESP32 board.

![Buzzer fixed on breadboard](../../../assets/1.0/BUZZER/circuit_2.png).

**Step 3:** Connect the negative pin to ground.

- Take another **jumper wire** and connect one end to the row containing the buzzer's negative pin (row 18).

Connect the other end to any **GND** pin on the ESP32 board.

![Buzzer fixed on breadboard](../../../assets/1.0/BUZZER/circuit_3.png).

_make sure you connect the ESP32 board usb blue cable to the ESP32 board_.

## PROGRAMMING

**Step 1:** Open your Arduino IDE. See how to set up here: [Getting Started](../../Getting Started/Arduino_IDE_Setup.md).

**Step 2:** Define your variables before the `setup()` function.

```cpp
int buzzer = 4;
```
*Explanation:* `int buzzer = 4;` creates a variable named `buzzer` and assigns it the value `4`. This means we will refer to GPIO 4 as `buzzer` throughout our code.

**Step 3:** Configure your pins inside the `void setup()` function.

```cpp
void setup() {
  pinMode(buzzer, OUTPUT);
}
```
*Explanation:* `pinMode(buzzer, OUTPUT);` sets GPIO 4 (the buzzer pin) as an OUTPUT, allowing the ESP32 to send power to the buzzer.

**Step 4:** Turn the buzzer on inside the `void loop()` function.

```cpp
void loop() {
  digitalWrite(buzzer, HIGH);
  delay(1000);
```
*Explanation:* `digitalWrite(buzzer, HIGH);` sends a signal to turn the buzzer ON, creating a sound. `delay(1000);` tells the ESP32 to wait for 1000 milliseconds (1 second) while the buzzer remains ON.

**Step 5:** Turn the buzzer off, continuing inside the `void loop()` function.

```cpp
  digitalWrite(buzzer, LOW);
  delay(1000);
}
```
*Explanation:* `digitalWrite(buzzer, LOW);` stops sending the signal, turning the buzzer OFF. `delay(1000);` creates a 1-second pause of silence before the loop starts over from the beginning.

**Step 6:** Save your code. _See the [Getting Started](../../Getting Started/Arduino_IDE_Setup.md) section_

**Step 7:** Select the ESP32 board and port _See the [Getting Started](../../Getting Started/Arduino_IDE_Setup.md) section:Selecting ESP32 board Type and Uploading your code_.

**Step 8:** Upload your code. _See the [Getting Started](../../Getting Started/Arduino_IDE_Setup.md) section:Selecting ESP32 board Type and Uploading your code_
